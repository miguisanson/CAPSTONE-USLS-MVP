PART A — GUIDELINES FOR WRITING A CONCEPT PAPER
====================================================================

A.1 PURPOSE OF THE CONCEPT PAPER

A concept paper is a brief but structured document that presents
a proposed research idea to the panel for evaluation during the
Title Defense (Gate 1 / Form 1). Each student must submit three
concept papers representing three distinct research topics or angles.
The panel selects or recommends one title from the three papers
for the student to develop as their thesis, dissertation, or
project paper.

The concept paper is not a full research proposal. It is a focused
summary that demonstrates the student's ability to identify a
researchable problem, situate it in existing literature, and propose
a feasible methodology.

A.2 LENGTH AND FORMAT

  Minimum length:   1,500 words (excluding references)
  Maximum length:   3,000 words (excluding references)
  Font:             Times New Roman, 12pt
  Line spacing:     Double
  Margins:          1 inch on all sides
  File format:      PDF (must be a text-readable PDF, not scanned image)

A.3 REQUIRED SECTIONS

Every concept paper must contain ALL of the following sections.
A paper missing any required section will be returned to the student
for revision before the Academic Coordinator can endorse Form 1.

  1. Title
  2. Background of the Study
  3. Statement of the Problem
  4. Significance of the Study
  5. Scope and Delimitation
  6. Theoretical or Conceptual Framework
  7. Proposed Methodology
  8. References

Each section is described in detail below.

A.4 SECTION DESCRIPTIONS AND REQUIREMENTS

SECTION 1 — TITLE
The title must be clear, specific, and reflective of the research
content. It should indicate the variables, population, and context
where applicable. Avoid vague or overly broad titles.

Good example:
  "Digital Wallet Adoption Among Micro-Enterprise Owners in Bacolod City:
   A Technology Acceptance Model Perspective"

Poor example:
  "A Study on Technology and Business"

SECTION 2 — BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
The background presents the context and rationale for the research.
It should:
  - Describe the situation or phenomenon being studied.
  - Present relevant statistics, trends, or prior research that
    establish the need for this study.
  - Establish the gap in existing knowledge that this research fills.
  - Be grounded in current and credible sources (within the last
    ten years, unless citing seminal works).

Minimum length: 300 words.

SECTION 3 — STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
This section states the central research problem and breaks it down
into specific, answerable research questions or objectives.

Requirements:
  - One (1) overarching research problem or central question.
  - Minimum of three (3) specific research questions or objectives
    derived from the central problem.
  - Questions must be specific, measurable, and researchable.
  - Avoid yes/no questions; use "what," "how," "to what extent,"
    or "what is the relationship between."

Example format:
  Central problem: This study investigates the relationship between
  financial literacy and savings behavior among public school teachers
  in Negros Occidental.

  Specific questions:
  1. What is the level of financial literacy of the respondents in terms
     of budgeting, investing, and debt management?
  2. What is the savings behavior of the respondents as measured by
     frequency of saving, amount saved, and savings instruments used?
  3. Is there a significant relationship between financial literacy and
     savings behavior of the respondents?

SECTION 4 — SIGNIFICANCE OF THE STUDY
This section explains the value and contribution of the research.
It should identify who will benefit from the findings and how.

Typical beneficiaries to address:
  - The institution or organization being studied
  - The profession or industry
  - Policy makers or administrators
  - Future researchers
  - The community or society

Each beneficiary group must be mentioned with a specific explanation
of how the research findings will benefit them.

SECTION 5 — SCOPE AND DELIMITATION
This section clearly defines what the study will and will not cover.

  Scope:       The specific variables, population, time period,
               and geographic coverage of the study.
  Delimitation: The conscious boundaries set by the researcher
               and the justification for those boundaries.

Example: "This study is limited to full-time faculty members of the
College of Business at USLS during the academic year 2025-2026.
Part-time faculty and administrative staff are excluded from the study."

SECTION 6 — THEORETICAL OR CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK
This section presents the theory or model that underpins the research.

  - Identify and explain the theory or model.
  - Cite the original source of the theory or model.
  - Explain how the theory applies to the current study.
  - Include a conceptual framework diagram if applicable (optional
    for the concept paper stage, required at proposal stage).

SECTION 7 — PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
This section outlines the research design and approach. It must
include all of the following:

  (a) Research design — Describe whether the study is quantitative,
      qualitative, or mixed methods. Identify the specific design
      (e.g., descriptive-correlational, phenomenological, case study,
      experimental, action research).

  (b) Participants or respondents — Who will be the subjects of the
      study? State the population and the sampling method.

  (c) Sampling procedure — How will participants be selected?
      (e.g., purposive sampling, stratified random sampling, total
      enumeration)

  (d) Data collection instruments — What tools will be used to gather
      data? (e.g., survey questionnaire, interview guide, observation
      checklist, document review)

  (e) Data analysis — How will the data be analyzed?
      Quantitative: state the statistical tools (e.g., mean, Pearson r,
      regression, chi-square).
      Qualitative: state the analysis approach (e.g., thematic analysis,
      content analysis, grounded theory coding).

SECTION 8 — REFERENCES
  - Minimum of ten (10) references.
  - At least seven (7) must be from peer-reviewed journals or
    academic sources.
  - At least five (5) must have been published within the last
    ten (10) years.
  - Use APA 7th edition format for all references.
  - References must match in-text citations.

PART B — CHECKLIST FOR CONCEPT PAPER COMPLIANCE
====================================================================

Use this checklist to verify that a concept paper meets minimum
requirements before Academic Coordinator endorsement.

ITEM                                                   REQUIRED   PRESENT
Title (clear, specific, reflects variables/context)       Yes     [ ]
Background of the Study (min. 300 words)                  Yes     [ ]
Background cites current sources (last 10 years)          Yes     [ ]
Background identifies research gap                        Yes     [ ]
Statement of the Problem (1 central + 3 specific)         Yes     [ ]
Research questions are specific and measurable            Yes     [ ]
Significance of the Study (identifies beneficiaries)      Yes     [ ]
Scope and Delimitation clearly stated                     Yes     [ ]
Theoretical or Conceptual Framework identified            Yes     [ ]
Theory or model cited with original source                Yes     [ ]
Proposed Methodology — research design stated             Yes     [ ]
Proposed Methodology — participants described             Yes     [ ]
Proposed Methodology — sampling procedure described       Yes     [ ]
Proposed Methodology — data instruments described         Yes     [ ]
Proposed Methodology — analysis approach described        Yes     [ ]
References — minimum 10 sources                           Yes     [ ]
References — minimum 7 peer-reviewed                      Yes     [ ]
References — minimum 5 within last 10 years               Yes     [ ]
References in APA 7th edition format                      Yes     [ ]
File submitted as text-readable PDF (not scanned)         Yes     [ ]
Length between 1,500 and 3,000 words (excl. references)  Yes     [ ]

RESULT OPTIONS:
  Compliant              — All required items present. Proceed to endorsement.
  Needs Revision         — One to three items missing or incomplete.
                           Return to student with specific feedback.
  Missing Requirements   — Four or more items missing or incomplete.
                           Return to student; endorsement cannot proceed.
